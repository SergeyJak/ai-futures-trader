# First collector audit — 2 November 2026

The new collector has useful observations, but the history is about 22 hours long. It does not establish a profitable strategy. Historical funding changes the selected strategy results only slightly; the short breakout still has essentially no gross price edge.

Snapshot: captured **2026-11-02 16:38:31 UTC**, analysis cutoff **16:37:31 UTC**. Source: the production application's read-only `/api/research-data` endpoints. The export contains 63 capped-window responses with SHA-256 hashes; inclusive boundary duplicates are verified identical and deduplicated. No production rows or trading parameters were changed.

## Data quality and collector fix

| Symbol | Quote samples | Missing 10s quote slots within observed range | Trade buckets | Flagged incomplete trade buckets | Funding settlements |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| BTCUSDT | 7,907 | 6 | 7,882 | 19 | 363 |
| ETHUSDT | 7,907 | 6 | 7,885 | 21 | 363 |
| SOLUSDT | 7,907 | 6 | 7,785 | 20 | 363 |

Quote coverage starts 1 November at 18:38:50 UTC. The largest quote gap is 70 seconds. Trade aggregates contain 2,602,258 accepted trades. Missing trade buckets can mean inactivity or data loss and are not filled with zeros. A continuous-stream flag is not proof that the exchange delivered every event.

The runtime recorded **16,332 dropped trade events** since its latest start; this is not a lifetime total or a statement about orders executed by a strategy. Accepted trades across symbols in a rolling 120-second window plateau at exactly **50,000** near 15:58–16:00 UTC. The observed peak over 40 seconds is 44,121 accepted trades; rejected trades cannot be reconstructed from these aggregates.

The previous collector retained duplicate IDs for 120 seconds after receipt. Its global cap is 50,000 IDs. Yet a trade is already permanently too late once its event bucket ends plus the 30-second allowance. Finalized IDs therefore crowded out live trades during bursts.

The fix expires IDs at that same bucket boundary, on ingestion and flush. It handles out-of-order bucket arrival and keeps active duplicate protection across reconnects. The 50,000-ID limit, bucket limit and incomplete-coverage markers remain. Extremely large bursts can still exhaust the bounded active cache; the fix does not promise a lossless feed. Existing incomplete data remains incomplete.

Regression: at 1,200 trades/second for 120 seconds, the old implementation accepted 50,000 of 144,000 unique trades. The fix accepts all 144,000 and rejects the 120 injected duplicates. Tests also exercise exact expiry, out-of-order timestamps, reconnect replays and genuine capacity loss. Independent review found no blocking issues.

| ETH | 0.000440% | 0.000444% | 0.120440% | 98.15% of samples |
| SOL | 0.010595% | 0.010716% | 0.130595% | 99.49% of samples |

BTC's mean exceeds its 99th percentile because rare wider-spread observations lift the mean. Fee assumptions remain **0.06% taker per side**, unverified for the actual account. A round trip crosses approximately half the spread on each side; this illustration adds one full mean spread, not two. For a $100 position, the illustrated fee-plus-spread expense is roughly $0.12–$0.13 before additional slippage and funding.

The earlier 0.18% round-trip model included 0.06% of slippage allowance on top of fees. These samples suggest that allowance is larger than typical quoted spread for small positions, but they do **not** establish actual fills, latency, market impact or execution during entry signals. No historical costs were replaced using this one-day sample. Maker fills and queue position are not verified.

## Historical funding sensitivity on unchanged trades

Settlement-rate histories run from 5 July through 2 November and are complete on the observed 8-hour schedule for these three symbols. Funding is estimated as `-direction × quantity × price_proxy × settlement_rate`; longs pay a positive rate and shorts receive it. The price proxy is the previous minute's trade-price close, because historical settlement mark prices are unavailable here.

| Selected 4h trend portfolio, risk1/gross3, July–October | 22 | +21.0491 | 1.1994 | +19.8497 | −0.2647 | **+19.5850** |
| Same portfolio, October standalone | 8 | −1.6019 | 0.3288 | −1.9307 | +0.0240 | **−1.9066** |
| Short breakout, July–October | 153 | −0.0225 | 4.3513 | −4.3738 | −0.0192 | **−4.3930** |

Amounts are USDT on the original fixed trade ledgers. For entries/exits timestamped exactly at a settlement, execution ordering is unknown. Including or excluding those boundary payments gives approximate net ranges of **+19.5522 to +19.5863**, **−1.9102 to −1.9053**, and **−4.4162 to −4.3893** respectively. These ranges concern boundary ordering only; they are not confidence intervals and do not bound mark-price approximation error.

All required rate/price observations were present for these diagnostics. This is an accounting sensitivity analysis, **not a funding-aware backtest rerun**: position sizing, liquidation, margin constraints and subsequent entries were not recalculated. It does not produce a new drawdown or monthly-return estimate. The historical strategies were already selected on these data; these results are not independent validation.

The main conclusions are unchanged:

- Funding is not the main loss source for these ledgers.
- The short breakout is approximately flat before fees/slippage. Cheaper execution alone does not establish a useful positive expectation.
- October's portfolio loses already on price movement.
- The profitable full-period portfolio remains a candidate with previously documented concentration in one BTC trade. Stable +10% per month is still unproven.

## Reproduction and validation

Python 3, standard library only:

```sh
python3 research/collector-audit/download.py --output /path/to/snapshot
python3 research/collector-audit/audit.py --snapshot /path/to/snapshot --study /path/to/strategy-study
python3 -m unittest discover -s research/collector-audit -p 'test_*.py'
```

The study directory is the earlier strategy-study dataset/ledgers from research PR #165: `data/*.json`, `portfolio-trades.csv`, `october-trades.csv`, `short-hold-trades.csv`. Reusing the original snapshot reproduces this report; a new output directory fetches a new observation period. The dated snapshot, original study inputs, scripts and detailed per-trade funding diagnostics are preserved in the accompanying research archive. `2026-11-02-summary.json` is the machine-readable report.

Validation: TypeScript build and full Node suite with PostgreSQL 16; focused collector tests; six Python funding-accounting tests; all snapshot SHA-256 checks; identical API overlap checks; original P&L decomposition reconciliation. No historical financial ledger is rewritten and no orders are submitted.
